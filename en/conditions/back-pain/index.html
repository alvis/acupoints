方 · A set of points

Back pain

Back pain visits most bodies sooner or later. This page gathers how traditional East Asian practice thought about the aching back, three classical stations it reached for — shenshu (BL23), weizhong (BL40), and huantiao (GB30) — and a gentle, honest home routine with its stop lines drawn clearly.

急

Get medical help now if

  • the pain began with a fall, an accident or a blow
  • numbness, tingling or weakness spreads into a leg or foot
  • bladder or bowel control changes, or the groin goes numb
  • fever comes with the pain, or it worsens night after night

Clinical research

  1. Randomised trial · Hsieh LL et al. · 2006

    Treatment of low back pain by acupressure and physical therapy: randomised controlled trial (BMJ, vol. 332)

    One hundred and twenty-nine people with chronic low back pain at one clinic in Taiwan. A month of acupressure lowered disability and pain more than physical therapy, and the gap held at six months. There was no sham group.

一

How Back Trouble Shows Up in Everyday Life

Back complaints rarely arrive with drama; more often they seep into ordinary routines. There is the stiffness on waking that eases after a few minutes of walking around the kitchen. There is the dull ache that gathers across the small of the back during a long afternoon at the desk, or halfway through a car journey. There is the sharp protest when a laundry basket is lifted awkwardly, or when a weekend of gardening follows months of sitting. Some backs grumble only after unusual effort; others keep up a low, background soreness for years, quiet on good days and loud on tired ones.

None of these patterns amounts to a diagnosis, and this page offers none. What they share is territory: the long muscles that flank the spine, the joints and discs between the vertebrae, and the web of tendons and ligaments tying the trunk to the hips and legs. Hours of sitting, long drives, heavy shoulder bags, sudden unaccustomed lifting, tense and clenched working days, and muscles left unconditioned by sedentary weeks all crowd the picture. Plenty of episodes fade as normal life resumes; some linger, worsen, or bring unfamiliar sensations — and those deserve a professional’s eyes before any self-care routine begins.

二

What Traditional Practice Reached For

Classical East Asian medicine drew the body as a landscape of channels, and no channel claims more of that landscape than the bladder meridian. Its pathway begins beside the eye, crosses the crown, and then descends the entire back — travelling not as one line but as two paired routes running parallel down either side of the spine, like tram tracks laid along the long back muscles. Nearly every station of the back belongs to these twin routes, which is one reason traditional teaching treats the whole back as bladder-channel country, mapped station by station from shoulders to sacrum.

Along the inner of those two routes, older texts arranged a second institution: the back-shu stations, where each organ’s activity was understood to gather closest to the surface. The lower back held particular meaning. Classical writings describe the loin as the residence of the kidney — the organ entrusted with the body’s deep reserves and the water it manages — so discomfort settling at the waistline was read against that residency. Tracing the spine itself, meanwhile, ran the governing vessel, the single line classical maps drew straight up the backbone, which made the spine a meeting ground of several selection traditions rather than one.

Selection followed location. A complaint centred beside the lumbar spine called for the station standing opposite it; an ache gripping the buttock or hip drew attention into that country itself; and tradition also liked to pair near with far, reasoning that a distant relay on the same channel could speak for the region it served. Three classical stations anchor this page: shenshu (BL23) level with the waist, weizhong (BL40) behind the knee, and huantiao (GB30) deep in the buttock. Together they sketch the old method — meet the pain where it lives, and send word along the channel besides.

三

Meeting the Three Stations

Shenshu (BL23) stands on the small of the back either side of the spinal seam: classically below the spinous process of the second lumbar vertebra, roughly navel height, one and a half cun lateral to the midline. In hand terms, find navel height, lay two fingers sideways against the seam, and the outer edge lands on shenshu’s broad muscle. The ground is friendly — sturdy bone beneath well-padded muscle, nothing fragile lying close under the skin. Tradition ranks shenshu as the kidney’s own address on the back, and lower back pain leads its recorded indications, which surprises nobody who has looked at the geography.

Weizhong (BL40) waits far from the ache, at the midpoint of the crease behind the knee, in the soft space between the two stout tendons that border it. Classical filing gives it two titles: he-sea point of the bladder channel, and one of the command stations tradition reaches for when the loin complains — the very region this channel walks down. Huantiao (GB30) occupies deep buttock country. Locating it asks you to lie on your side with the upper knee bent, find the junction of the outer and middle thirds of the line from the bony prominence at the side of the hip to the hollow near the tailbone, and settle into the flesh overlying the joint; its recorded indications speak of pain of the lower back and hip together.

Why send a knee point to answer a back complaint? Traditional reasoning treated channels as continuous circuits rather than scattered addresses: a point far downstream could still stand duty for territory upstream, much as a lighthouse guides ships that never touch its shore. Pairing a local station with a distant one also gave practice a rhythm — settle the neighbourhood first, then steady the current — and it explains why old case discussions move so freely between waist, hip, and knee. None of this asks the reader to accept the physiology; it simply reports the map the tradition worked from.

四

A Gentle Routine, With Its Stop Lines Drawn

Set aside ten unhurried minutes somewhere warm. Sit upright on a firm chair with the low back supported, or kneel resting forward on cushions — whichever lets the waist soften. Begin with contact alone: rest both palms flat on the lower back and breathe slowly, letting warmth arrive before pressure does. Then find shenshu at navel height, two fingers out from the seam, and make small slow circles there with the pads of two fingers — pleasant dullness or spreading warmth is the target sensation, never sharpness. One to three minutes suffices; more is not better.

Next come the two distant relays. Standing with the knees slightly bent, hook the thumbs of both hands into the crease behind each knee and press weizhong gently — briefly, since vessels and nerves travel that corridor, a light hold lasting a few breaths is plenty. For huantiao, lie on your side with the upper knee drawn up and sink the flat of a fist or the heel of the palm into the buttock slowly, stopping at whatever depth stays comfortable; the spot sits deep, so patience beats force. Close the session as it opened: palms flat on the loins, a few slow breaths, done. Once or twice daily is a reasonable rhythm, and skipping days costs nothing.

The stop lines matter more than the technique. Never press over broken, bruised, or inflamed skin; set the whole practice aside during an acute flare, and for a stretch after any fall or injury until a clinician has cleared ordinary movement. Sharp, electric, or radiating sensation ends the session immediately. Use finger pads and palms only — no elbows, knuckles, or tools. If you are pregnant, live with a chronic condition such as osteoporosis or diagnosed disc disease, or take medication that affects bleeding, ask a qualified practitioner before beginning, and treat the first sessions as experiments in gentleness rather than tests of endurance.

五

Honest Context: Habits, Prevention, and Limits

No point outruns its habits. Backs generally reward variety — alternating sitting and walking through the day, standing up at least hourly during desk-bound stretches, warming up before the garden or the gym, lifting loads close to the body with the legs doing the work. Sleep deserves attention too: a mattress neither sagging nor slab-stiff, a pillow that keeps the neck in line, and side-lying with a cushion between the knees suit many people. Stress earns a mention as well, since clenched working days deposit their tension squarely across the shoulders and loin. Nothing on this list is exotic; nearly all of it is free.

What self-applied pressure can honestly offer sits inside modest bounds: minutes of deliberate slowing, warmer-feeling muscles, and a small ritual of attention to a neglected region. Reviews suggest acupressure may ease low back pain and disability when trained practitioners do the pressing, and this page promises nothing beyond comfort. Think of the routine as maintenance for a working structure, not repair of a broken one: habits and gentle pressure keep company with medical care; they do not replace it. Readers hoping for a firm promise of relief will not find one here — only a practice many people find steadying while their back settles.

六

Clear Boundaries: When Self-Care Is Not the Answer

Certain companions of back pain change everything, and none of them belong with a pressure routine. Seek medical assessment promptly — before trying self-care, not after it fails — for pain that begins with a fall, accident, or direct blow; numbness, tingling, or spreading weakness moving into a leg or foot, including a leg that buckles or a foot that drags; any change in bladder or bowel control; numbness around the groin or inner thighs; fever arriving alongside back pain; unexplained weight loss alongside the pain; or pain that is severe, worsening night after night, or unexplained by anything you did. These signals need trained eyes urgently, and pressing points first spends time that matters.

For everyone else, honesty about scope serves better than enthusiasm. This page cannot diagnose why a back hurts, cannot replace examination, and cannot promise relief; it can offer a way of knowing the terrain, three well-recorded stations, and a routine gentle enough to set down without regret. Used alongside — never instead of — professional care, it gives many people a sense of agency during weeks that would otherwise feel like waiting. Persistent symptoms belong with a licensed practitioner — Western medicine or East Asian medicine, whichever suits the complaint.

停

Self-care limits

Educational content only. Self-care here means light fingertip and palm contact only — no tools, no elbows — on intact skin, skipped entirely during acute flares and until ordinary movement is cleared after any recent injury. Stop at once on sharp, electric, or radiating sensation. Pregnancy, osteoporosis, disc problems, or blood-thinning medication call for a qualified practitioner first. The red-flag symptoms described above go to medical assessment promptly. Self-acupressure is educational self-care, not medical care. Stop if a sensation becomes sharp, and seek professional help for persistent symptoms.

問

Frequently asked

Is it safe to press shenshu, weizhong, or huantiao at home?
For healthy adults, light fingertip work on these surfaces is generally a gentle practice: shenshu sits on padded muscle, weizhong takes only brief soft holds, and huantiao answers best to patient palm pressure rather than pointed force. Keep everything comfortable, avoid irritated skin, and stop at once on sharp or electric sensation. Pregnancy, osteoporosis, disc problems, blood-thinning medication, or any recent injury all argue for consulting a qualified practitioner first.
Why would a knee point help a back problem?
Because classical medicine treated channels as connected circuits rather than isolated spots. Weizhong sits on the bladder meridian — the very channel that walks down the whole back — and tradition files it among the command stations for the loin. The old image is of a circuit answered at a distance: press the relay, and the region it serves hears about it. Whether or not one accepts the framework, pairing local and distant points is simply how the tradition organised its choices.
How often, and how long, should a session last?
Short and regular beats long and rare. One to three minutes at each station, once or twice daily, is a reasonable home rhythm, and missing days costs nothing. Quality matters more than duration: warm hands, unhurried breathing, pressure that stays pleasant throughout. If a session leaves the area sore for hours afterwards, the pressure was too firm — halve it next time, or rest the routine for a day or two.
Can acupressure fix my back pain?
No honest page can promise that, and this one does not try. Self-acupressure is best understood as comfort care: a few minutes of warmth, touch, and deliberate slowing that some backs seem to appreciate. It is not diagnosis, not treatment, and not a substitute for examination. If pain persists beyond a few weeks, returns again and again, or begins travelling into a leg, put the question to a clinician; the routine can wait, or accompany, but never replace that conversation.
When does back pain need a professional rather than self-care?
Promptly, whenever warning signs appear: pain following a fall or blow; numbness, tingling, or weakness spreading into a leg; changes in bladder or bowel control; numbness around the groin; fever alongside back pain; or severe, worsening, or unexplained pain. Beyond emergencies, lingering discomfort that outlasts a couple of weeks also deserves assessment — not necessarily alarming, simply wiser. Between visits, gentle routines like the one above remain available, provided every stop line stays respected.
