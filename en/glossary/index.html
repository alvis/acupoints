Cun

cùn·noun · a unit of lengthAlso: 同身寸 tóngshēn cùn, the body’s own inch
寸
義Means

The cun is East Asian medicine’s body-proportional unit of distance: counted not in tape-measure centimetres but in widths taken from the person being measured. This page explains where the idea comes from, how the finger-width shortcuts work, and how to read the cun counts you will meet across this site.

一言In one line

Your own thumb’s width: the ruler every location on this site is written in.

見Meet it

In nearly every location line, such as “2 cun above the wrist crease”. Three worked examples:

  • 內關NeiguanPC6leaf →2 cun above the wrist crease
  • 三陰交SanyinjiaoSP6leaf →3 cun above the tip of the inner anklebone
  • 足三里ZusanliST36leaf →3 cun below the hollow under the outer knee
參See also
問 · Asked oftenIs a cun just another word for a centimetre?No. The syllable cun also names an ordinary market unit of length in China — a little over three centimetres — but that fixed measure is not what this site means. Every cun on our point pages is proportional: a slice of the reader’s own body, taken from thumb widths and stacked fingers, convertible to no ruler at all.
